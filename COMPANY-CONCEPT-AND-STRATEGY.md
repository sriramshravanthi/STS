## 1. COMPANY CONCEPT

### 1.1 The core promise

> **We help businesses identify, solve, implement, and manage their AI-related problems.**

A client does not need to arrive knowing what they want built. They arrive with a
problem — a process that costs too much, a backlog nobody can clear, a board asking
what the AI plan is, a pilot that stalled, a team that lacks the skills, or a role
they cannot fill. One intake, one owner, one path to the outcome.

### 1.2 Why this shape of company

The market has fragmented the AI value chain across four different vendor types,
and the client absorbs every seam:

| The client needs | Today they hire | The seam that hurts |
|---|---|---|
| To know what to build | A strategy consultancy | Ends at the recommendation |
| To have it built | A dev shop or SI | Builds what it is told, right or wrong |
| Their people to run it | A training vendor | Generic curriculum, not their system |
| People to staff it | A staffing agency | Screens on résumés, not demonstrated skill |

NorthKey AI collapses all four into one accountable partner. That integration is
the entire business thesis — the training is built on the system we shipped, and
the talent we place has been assessed and upskilled by us, on our own platform.

### 1.3 The nine-step client journey

```
1  Understand the problem      →  free triage session, no deck
2  Analyze requirements        →  data, systems, risk, feasibility
3  Identify the AI solution    →  or say plainly that AI is not the answer
4  Provide consultants/engineers →  named, senior, US-based
5  Develop & implement         →  in the client's repo, client's cloud
6  Test & deploy               →  evaluations, security review, rollout
7  Train the client's employees →  on the system we just built
8  Provide ongoing support     →  managed AI operations
9  Provide contract AI professionals →  when the client wants to own it
```

### 1.4 The ecosystem (this is the website's closing message)

```
Businesses   → bring AI problems
Consultants  → analyze the problems
AI Engineers → build the solutions
Trainers     → upskill the client's people
Assessments  → measure real skill, objectively
Talent Team  → identify qualified professionals
Recruitment  → connect that talent to clients
Contracts    → deliver professionals and projects
Support      → maintain and scale what was built
```

Each arrow feeds the next. Assessments qualify talent *and* generate training
demand. Delivery projects generate training and staffing demand. Training produces
assessed candidates for the talent pool. This is a flywheel, not a service menu.

### 1.5 Who we serve

| Segment | Profile | Primary need |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise | 2,000+ staff, US | Governance, integration, staff augmentation |
| Mid-market | $50M–$2B revenue | End-to-end delivery, no internal AI org |
| Startups | Seed–Series B | Fractional architecture, agent/LLM build |
| Professionals | Individual learners, worldwide | Courses, assessments, career path, placement |
| Organizations | Government, education, nonprofit | Literacy, responsible AI, procurement support |

### 1.6 Who we are not

- Not a résumé-forwarding staffing agency. Every candidate is assessed by us.
- Not a reseller. We take no margin on cloud, license, or model spend.
- Not a slide factory. The majority of the firm should be builders.
- Not a research lab. We apply models; we do not train foundation models.

NEXT STEP        Retake window, or progress to the next level
```

### 8.3 Integrity and honesty rules

- **No certification claim without psychometric review.** A "certificate of
  completion" is honest; a "certification" implies a standard behind it. Do not
  blur the two.
- Item bank target: 60+ reviewed items per track/level before commercial launch.
- Proctoring required for any result used in a hiring decision.
- The site's public assessment is explicitly labeled a **sample**, not a
  certification, and the working demo in `index.html` says so.

### 8.4 Business role

The assessment engine is the hinge of the whole model. It generates learner demand
(gaps → courses), qualifies the talent pool (score → placement readiness), and gives
clients objective evidence about candidates that no résumé provides.

### 10.4 Compliance — highest-risk division

> Staffing and recruitment are regulated activities and carry the most legal
> exposure in this business model. Before operating, resolve with counsel:

> (W-2 vs. 1099 vs. corp-to-corp); co-employment exposure in staff augmentation;

> **adverse-impact testing on any AI-assisted screening**; pay-transparency laws
> in states that have them (job postings); NYC Local Law 144-style bias-audit
> rules for automated employment decision tools; background-check (FCRA) process;
> and non-solicitation and conversion-fee terms in client contracts.

> The AI Advisor provides preliminary guidance only. It is not a proposal, a quote,
> or professional advice. A human consultant reviews every actual project
> requirement before any recommendation is made.

### 17.4 Implementation phases

**Phase 1 (built):** deterministic rule-based recommender running client-side. No
data leaves the browser until the visitor submits the form. Fast, free, private.
**Phase 2:** LLM-backed conversational advisor behind an authenticated backend,
with retrieval over the real service catalog, PII redaction, logging, rate
limiting, and the same human-review disclaimer.

### 19.1 Sequencing — do not launch all ten

```

Launching a course platform and an assessment engine before the consulting business
pays the bills is the single most likely way this company fails. Services first.

## 22. WEBSITE UI/UX DESIGN

### 22.1 Direction

**"Technical document, premium print."** An enterprise-credible editorial system:
numbered sections, hairline rules, sharp corners, generous vertical rhythm, and
typography doing the work. Deliberately *not* the default AI-startup aesthetic.

**Explicitly avoided:** purple-to-blue gradients, glassmorphism, floating orbs,
glowing neural-network hero art, robot imagery, generic circuit-board graphics,
stock photos of people pointing at screens, neon-on-black, rounded-everything.

### 22.2 Design tokens

| Token | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|
| `--bg` | `#F4F2EC` warm bone | `#0E1114` |
| `--bg-2` | `#EDEAE1` | `#161A1F` |
| `--ink` | `#0F1216` | `#EDEAE1` |
| `--ink-2` | `#4C525A` | `#A3A9B2` |
| `--ink-3` | `#7E858E` | `#6E747C` |
| `--rule` | `#D9D4C7` | `#262B32` |
| `--accent` | `#1C5D4A` deep pine | `#5FBFA0` |
| `--accent-2` | `#C0521B` burnt orange | `#E08046` |

Accent discipline: the accent colors touch roughly 5% of the surface — rules,
numerals, underlines, one button. Never large fills.

### 22.3 Typography

| Role | Face | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Newsreader** | Hero and section headings, tight tracking, never bold-blocky |
| Body / UI | **Instrument Sans** | All body copy, navigation, buttons, forms |
| Mono | **IBM Plex Mono** | Section numbers, eyebrow labels, metrics, code |

Scale: hero `clamp(2.75rem, 7vw, 5.5rem)` · h2 `clamp(2rem, 4vw, 3rem)` ·
h3 `1.375rem` · body `1.0625rem` · mono label `0.6875rem` uppercase, `0.14em` tracking.

### 22.4 Layout and motion

12-column feel with visible hairline section rules · asymmetric 5/7 and 4/8 splits ·
`border-radius: 2px` maximum · 96–140px desktop section padding · subtle SVG grain
overlay · no drop shadows (depth from rules and value).

Motion: one staggered page-load reveal on the hero; `IntersectionObserver`
scroll reveals firing once each; hover underlines that draw in; accent rule that
slides on cards. **All motion disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.**

### 22.5 Accessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA target · 4.5:1 body contrast in both themes · visible accent focus
rings · full keyboard operation including the advisor and assessment · semantic
landmarks and one `<h1>` · `aria-live` on dynamic results · labeled form controls
with inline error text · no horizontal page scroll at any width · wide tables
scroll inside their own container · dark mode by `prefers-color-scheme` and by
persistent toggle.

### 24.2 Verification checklist before launch

**Entity**
- [ ] U.S. business registration — entity type and state, with an attorney and CPA
- [ ] Secretary of State business-name availability search
- [ ] EIN, business bank account, registered agent
- [ ] Business insurance: professional liability (E&O) is essential for consulting;
      add general liability, cyber liability, and workers' comp where required

**Brand**
- [ ] USPTO trademark search (TESS), classes 035 and 042 — then file
- [ ] Domain availability and acquisition
- [ ] Social handle availability
- [ ] Common-law use search

**Contracts** (all attorney-drafted)
- [ ] MSA · SOW template · NDA (mutual) · IP assignment · Data processing agreement
- [ ] Staffing/placement agreement with conversion fees and non-solicit terms
- [ ] Independent contractor agreements · Course terms and refund policy
- [ ] Assessment terms and integrity policy

**Regulatory**
- [ ] Recruitment/staffing agency licensing per operating state
- [ ] Worker classification (W-2 / 1099 / C2C) and co-employment analysis
- [ ] I-9, E-Verify, EEOC, FCRA background-check compliance
- [ ] **Adverse-impact testing on any AI-assisted candidate screening**, plus
      NYC Local Law 144-style bias-audit and notice rules where applicable
- [ ] Pay-transparency requirements in job postings
- [ ] Whether the state regulates paid instruction as a private career school
- [ ] Sales tax on digital courses — varies by state
- [ ] Applicable federal, state, and local tax and employment requirements

**Privacy and site**
- [ ] Privacy policy, terms of service, cookie notice
- [ ] CCPA/CPRA and other state privacy laws; GDPR if serving EU learners
- [ ] Accessibility statement (ADA/WCAG exposure on public-facing sites)
- [ ] Security: MFA, encryption at rest and in transit, retention and deletion policy,
      incident response plan, vendor due diligence
- [ ] Publish our own AI use policy at `/legal/ai-use-policy` — a trust asset

### 24.3 Claim discipline

| Never say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "SOC 2 certified" (until audited) | "Security practices aligned to [framework]" |
| "Trusted by Fortune 500 companies" | "Built for enterprise requirements" |

## 25. FUTURE SCALABILITY PLAN

### 25.1 Roadmap

**Phase 1 — Prove the service (months 0–6)**
Static marketing site · founder-led sales · consulting and development only ·
first 3 clients · forms to CRM · convert illustrative case studies to real ones.
*Success: 3 paying clients, positive cash, 1 published real case study.*

**Phase 2 — Add recurring revenue (months 6–12)**
Corporate training sold into existing clients · contract staffing · managed services ·
first 5 Academy courses · real assessment item bank · client portal v1.
*Success: 30%+ of revenue recurring, 10+ assessed candidates placed.*

**Phase 3 — Platform (months 12–24)**
Full Academy with student portal · public assessment platform · talent portal ·
career center · admin dashboard · LLM-backed AI Advisor · content engine at scale.
*Success: Academy self-sustaining, talent pool of 500+ assessed professionals.*

**Phase 4 — Products and scale (months 24+)**
Proprietary SaaS from repeated delivery patterns · partner and reseller channel ·
international delivery for the Academy · possible geographic or vertical specialization.

### 25.2 What must be built to be scalable, not bespoke

| Area | Scalable form |
|---|---|
| Delivery | Reusable accelerators (RAG scaffold, agent framework, eval harness) |
| Sales | Documented qualification, standard SOW templates, published pricing bands |
| Training | Recorded once, sold many times; live sessions as the premium tier |
| Assessment | Item bank with statistics, auto-scored, auto-reported |
| Talent | Assessment-driven matching, not manual résumé review |
| Knowledge | Every project produces a reusable pattern in an internal library |

### 25.3 The risks that actually kill this business

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Doing six things badly instead of two well | **Critical** | Enforce the phase sequence in §19.1 |
| Cash burned on platform before services pay | **Critical** | Services fund the platform. Never the reverse. |
| Key-person dependency on the founder | High | Document the method early; hire a delivery lead by client 5 |
| Staffing/employment law exposure | High | Counsel before the first placement |
| Talent cold-start (no pool, no clients) | High | Free assessment builds the pool before demand exists |
| Commoditization of AI implementation | Medium | Move up into governance and down into managed services |
| Model/vendor churn breaking delivered systems | Medium | Model-agnostic architecture, eval harnesses catch regressions |
| Making a claim we cannot support | Medium | The claim discipline in §24.3, enforced in review |

### 25.4 The one-sentence strategy

> Sell consulting and development to fund a training and assessment platform that
> supplies a talent business — and let each division create demand for the next.
