The NorthKey Method

Three beats, eight phases, and a written exit criterion at every one. You can stop at the end of any phase and keep everything produced up to that point — which is the opposite of how most AI engagements are sold.

  • Beat oneProveEstablish that the problem is real, the data supports it, and the money is there. Two phases, and a genuine right to walk away at the end of them.
  • Beat twoProduceDesign, build, and deploy the working system — in your repository, on your cloud, with evaluations running in CI from the first commit.
  • Beat threePass onTrain your people on this system, support it while they take it over, then scale to the next use case. Success is you not needing us.

Six questions about your organization, scored across the six dimensions that decide whether AI reaches production. Free, instant, and nothing you enter leaves your browser. You get a maturity level, a scored profile, your binding constraint, and the engagement that actually fits where you are.

The six dimensions

  • 01StrategyIs there a funded, owned business case — or an interest in AI?
  • 02DataCan you actually reach, trust, and govern the data a use case needs?
  • 03TechnologyDoes your platform let you ship and operate a model safely?

  • 05GovernanceIs there a path to approval, or does legal stop everything?

Most organizations score unevenly. The lowest dimension is almost always the binding constraint — and spending on any other one first is how AI budgets get wasted.

Document-heavy intake in a regulated operation

Problem

Illustrative Example

Knowledge scattered across a decade of internal content

Problem

Analysis
Content inventory, permission model review, question sampling from the help desk, and a retrieval quality baseline before any build.
AI Solution
Permission-aware retrieval-augmented generation with mandatory citations and an evaluation set built from real historical questions.
Implementation
Retrieval quality proven against the evaluation set before the generation layer is exposed to any user.
Technology
Chunking and embedding pipeline, vector store, re-ranking, citation enforcement, automated evals in CI.

Illustrative Example

A multi-step operational process nobody can staff

Problem
A recurring workflow requires reading a request, gathering context from three systems, drafting a response, and routing it. Volume exceeds capacity.
Analysis
Task decomposition, error-cost analysis per step, and a decision on which steps may run autonomously and which require a human gate.
AI Solution
An agent with scoped tool access, a defined blast radius, human approval on consequential actions, and full step-level tracing.
Implementation
Shadow mode first — the agent proposes, humans decide — until the evaluation suite justifies widening autonomy.
Technology
Agent orchestration, tool/API integration, guardrails, observability, cost and latency budgets, rollback path.
Result
Measured on throughput, accuracy versus the human baseline, and escalation rate. Reported per engagement.

What we don't claim

Look at any competitor's AI page and you will find the same wall: analyst badges, client logos, a partner grid, and a round number of projects delivered. Most of it is real. Some of it is decoration. None of it is verifiable by you in the moment you are reading it.

So here is the inverse — stated plainly, because a firm that will tell you what it cannot do is more useful than one that tells you it can do everything.

  • No analyst recognition We are not a Gartner, Forrester, IDC, HFS, or Everest-rated firm. Instead Judge the method. It is published in full on this page, including the exit rights, so you can evaluate it before spending anything.
  • No certifications yet No SOC 2, no ISO 42001, no HIPAA attestation. We will not display a badge we have not earned. Instead Security and governance practices are described concretely and can be reviewed under NDA before any engagement.
  • No client logos No logo wall, no named references, no testimonials. Instead Case studies here are labelled illustrative solution patterns. Real ones will be published only with written client approval.
  • No partner tier claims We are not advertising ourselves as an AWS, Microsoft, Google, Databricks, or Snowflake partner. Instead We build on all of them and take zero margin on any of them — which is precisely why our recommendation is worth something.
  • No performance statistics No "40% faster", no "500+ projects delivered", no accuracy percentages. Instead Every engagement defines its own success metric in Phase 01, and that number is reported to you — not to a marketing page.
  • No certification claims Our assessments issue certificates of completion, not certifications. Instead The distinction is real: a certification requires a psychometrically reviewed item bank and proctoring. We will say so when that exists.

Why this is on the website

The claims are the product.

An AI consultancy sells judgement. If the first judgement you see is a marketing page willing to overstate, that tells you what the second one will look like when a pilot is not working and there is a Phase 03 invoice at stake.

Our rule is simple, and it applies internally as well as here: if a claim would be uncomfortable to defend under oath, it does not get published. That rule is why this section exists — and it is the same rule that makes a "no-go" recommendation in Phase 02 possible.

Frequently asked questions

What does an AI consulting company actually do?
An AI consulting company helps an organization work out where artificial intelligence will produce a business result, whether its data and systems can support that, what it will cost to build and run, and what risk it carries. We do that — and then, unlike a pure advisory firm, we also build the solution, train your staff on it, and supply the AI professionals to run it.
How is this different from hiring a strategy firm and a development shop separately?
The seam between them is where most AI programs die. A strategy firm's recommendation is handed to a development shop that was not in the room when the business case was written, and the translation loss is expensive. Here the person who wrote the business case sits in the build reviews. One team, one accountable owner, one number.
We already tried an AI pilot and it stalled. Can you help?
That is one of the most common reasons clients come to us. Pilots usually stall for one of four reasons: no real business case, no data foundation, no governance path to approval, or no owner once the vendor left. The first thing we do is work out which of those it was — because the fix is different in each case.
What if AI is not the right answer to our problem?
Then we tell you, and we say so during the assessment rather than after you have paid for a build. Sometimes the answer is a process change, better reporting, or conventional software. That is a legitimate outcome of the assessment phase and a far cheaper one than the alternative.
Who owns the code and the intellectual property?
You do, from day one. Everything is built in your repository, under your license, with no license-back to us. Source code, prompts, evaluation sets, infrastructure-as-code, and documentation are all yours. Handover, documentation, and a recorded walkthrough are in scope, not a change order.
Which AI models and cloud platforms do you work with?
We are deliberately model-agnostic and cloud-agnostic, and we take no reseller margin on software, cloud, or model spend. The model is chosen per use case on the basis of evaluation results, cost, latency, and data-residency requirements — not a partnership agreement.
Do you provide AI engineers on contract?
Yes. Short-term contracts, long-term contracts, project-based work, staff augmentation, and dedicated AI teams. Every professional is assessed and upskilled by us before presentation, and you see named candidates before any contract is signed.
How is your AI training different from an online course platform?
For individuals, the Academy is a structured curriculum from beginner to advanced with assessments that name your specific gaps. For enterprises, the difference is bigger: when we have built a system for you, the training is delivered on that system — your data, your workflows, your guardrails — because we built it and know exactly how it behaves.
Are your assessments a certification?
No. We issue certificates of completion, and we are precise about the distinction. A certification implies an independently validated standard behind it, with a psychometrically reviewed item bank and proctoring. We will not describe our assessments as certifications until that standard is genuinely met.
How do you price engagements?
Fixed fee per phase, with scope and exit criteria written down before work begins. You approve each phase before it starts and can stop at any gate, keeping everything produced to that point. Contract staffing is priced per engagement.

How quickly can you start?
A triage session is usually available within a few business days. Assessment and advisory work typically starts within two to three weeks of a signed scope. Contract staffing depends on the role — we present candidates from the assessed talent pool rather than starting a search from zero.
